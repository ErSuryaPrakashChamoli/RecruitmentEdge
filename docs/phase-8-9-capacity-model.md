**Benchmark limits:**

### 1.3 BENCHMARK scaled tiers (`hrms_p89_bench`)

- **Method:** each requisition slice is cloned with an id offset of k × 10,000,000. FKs inside the slice keep the offset; unique codes are suffixed; candidate emails are prefixed. `ANALYZE` was run after each tier.
- **500k:** 500,315 candidates; 500,310 applications; 1,355,895 stage histories; 2,600 open requisitions; 250,805 communications.
- **1M:** 1,000,630 candidates; 1,000,620 applications; 2,711,790 stage histories; 5,200 open requisitions; 501,610 communications.
- **Server:** a development MySQL 8.4 with the default 128 MB buffer pool. Above 100k the scans are disk-bound, so absolute times are pessimistic; growth between tiers is the signal (`phase-8-9-performance.md` §1).
- **Execution:** the 500k probe stopped at the first metric failure. Its remaining operations were measured after the 1M run, by trimming the same database back to 500k. At 1M, `pipeline.time_in_stage` was stopped after 22.4 minutes.
- The database was dropped after measurement. No permanent benchmark data remains.

## 2. Tier model

### 2.1 Candidates (open requisitions scale with them: ≈ 1 per 192 candidates in the benchmark)

| Tier | Interactive (scoped lists, search) | Organisation-wide analytics | Hourly / nightly background | Main constraint |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10k (PROJECTED) | fast | seconds | fine | none observed |
| 50k (PROJECTED) | scoped list ≈ 0.25 s | 365-day organisation-wide set ≈ 25 s | fine | analytics |
| 100k (BENCHMARK) | scoped list 0.47–0.72 s; search 0.15–0.6 s | 365-day set **53.6 s**; this month 3.4 s | `dispatch-alerts` likely exceeds memory (585 MB vs 256M) | analytics; alert sweep memory |
| 250k (PROJECTED) | scoped list ≈ 1.5 s | this month ≈ 12 s; `time_in_stage` beyond the 120 s execution limit | intelligence refresh ≈ 17 min cold | full scans |
| 500k (BENCHMARK) | scoped list **3.1–3.5 s**; search up to **4.0 s** | this month **26 s**; 365-day set **fails** (MySQL 1390) | refresh ≈ 35 min cold | full scans; metric defects; scheduler cadence |
| 1M (BENCHMARK) | scoped list **≈ 7 s**; scoped search **8.0 s** | this month **83 s**; metrics exceed 256 MB; `time_in_stage` never completes | refresh > 1 h, **misses its cadence** (PROJECTED) | everything that scans or hydrates whole sets |

Interactive paths that use an index do not degrade with the candidate count:
- duplicate detection;
- candidate 360 (timeline, audit, communications);
- notification centre;
- portal dashboard;
- outcome due query.

These stayed in the 1–4 ms range at both 100k and 500k (BENCHMARK).

### 2.2 Open requisitions (PROJECTED from the Phase 8.7 measurement: 414 s cold / 19.5 s warm at 520)

| Open requisitions | `intelligence:refresh` cold | warm | Health snapshots / year | Evidence rows / year | Storage / year |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 520 (BENCHMARK) | 414 s | 19.5 s | ≤ 0.76M | ≤ 16M | ≈ 12 GB |
| 1,000 | ≈ 13 min | ≈ 38 s | ≤ 1.46M | ≤ 30M | ≈ 24 GB |
| 5,000 | ≈ 67 min (**exceeds hourly**) | ≈ 3 min | ≤ 7.3M | ≤ 152M | ≈ 120 GB |
| 10,000 | ≈ 133 min | ≈ 6 min | ≤ 14.6M | ≤ 303M | ≈ 240 GB |

**How the snapshot figures are derived:**
- `HiringHealthService` writes a new snapshot when the current one is older than 6 h. The hourly refresh therefore produces up to 4 per open requisition per day.
- Each snapshot carries a ≈ 3.6 KB metrics JSON and ≈ 21 evidence rows (BENCHMARK averages). Superseded snapshots are kept by design (time series).
- That is about **66 KB per open-requisition-day**, with nothing pruned (P89-PERF-026).
- These are upper bounds for a steady open count.
- Whether superseded snapshots must be kept is a product / legal question, not engineering's (D8.9-024).

**Other requisition-driven costs:**
- Risk Radar scan: 1.9 s / 123 queries for one requisition with 555 applications (BENCHMARK, 100k).
- `positionHealth` loads every open requisition with an application count, 3–5× per dashboard load.
- Talent signals: up to 200 applications × ~6 queries per requisition per run.

**Database size (PROJECTED).** Core recruitment tables plus audit come to about **1 GB at 100k** and **8–10 GB at 1M** at benchmark row widths.

That is not large for MySQL. **Size is not the 1M problem.** The problems are query shape (full scans, correlated subqueries, per-request recomputation) and the intelligence time series of §2.2, which can outgrow the core data within a year.

**Files (PROJECTED):** ≈ 1 TB at 1M candidates (PF-88-07). Export files and offer letters are never deleted.
